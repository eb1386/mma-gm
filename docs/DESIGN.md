# Design decisions

Every technically ambiguous requirement, and the production decision taken for it.

---

## 1. The real roster stops at the official rankings

**Ambiguity.** "Use genuine source data for real fighters" against "do not import every
fighter appearing in the complete historical directory" against terms of service, robots
rules and rate limits.

**Decision.** The only production source is ufc.com. Its `robots.txt` publishes
`crawl-delay: 15` and `Disallow: /athletes/all?*`. Honouring both means the full athlete
directory cannot be crawled, and that `/rankings` plus `/athlete/<slug>` are the compliant
surface. The real roster is therefore every champion plus the ranked one through fifteen in
each of the eight men's divisions: 128 athletes, ingested with zero gaps.

**Why not UFCStats.** It now serves a JavaScript proof of work bot wall. Bypassing a
deliberate anti automation control was not acceptable. `UFCStatsAdapter` exists in the
provider layer and reports its capabilities as unavailable, which is the honest state.

**Consequence and how it is handled.** Unranked roster depth is not available from a
compliant source. Rather than inventing people and presenting them as real, the world is
filled with clearly labelled fictional fighters carrying `isRealPerson: false`, a
`fictional` tag on every table row, and an origin filter on the roster page. A division
with 16 real ranked fighters and 44 fictional ones is honest about which is which on every
screen.

---

## 2. Contracts are always simulated

**Ambiguity.** The game needs contracts, but real fighter pay is not public.

**Decision.** Every contract carries `isSimulated: true` and a visible note stating that
real terms are not public and that every figure is generated by the game. No pay figure is
ever presented as reported. The contract valuation model deliberately excludes Ovr and
prices leverage from ranking, championship status, pound for pound standing, drawing power,
finish rate, recent form, reliability, activity, remaining career projection and
promotional experience. That is why a popular finisher outside the top ten can out earn a
technically superior but unmarketable contender.

---

## 3. Dates of birth are unknown, not guessed

Official athlete profiles publish an age, not a date of birth. Storing a fabricated
birthday would be inventing data. `birthDate` is `null` for every real fighter,
`ageAtSnapshot` holds the sourced age, and `ageOn()` falls back to it. The fighter page
shows **Unknown** for date of birth. The snapshot validation report counts every field the
source did not publish.

---

## 4. Ranking as the opponent adjustment channel

**Ambiguity.** The requirement asks for opponent adjustment, but the compliant source
exposes no bout by bout opponent data.

**Decision.** A ranking is the accumulated verdict on who a fighter has beaten, which is
precisely the information rate statistics lack. It is used as an explicit second evidence
channel and is documented as a proxy rather than presented as true opponent adjustment.
The two channels are blended, so neither a statistical outlier with no ranking nor a highly
ranked fighter with poor underlying numbers escapes scrutiny.

Rankings themselves never read Ovr. `src/core/world/rankings.ts` is a pure results system.

---

## 5. Cage minutes as the sample size

Rather than counting fights, the pipeline recovers cumulative cage minutes from
`sigStrLanded / sigStrLandedPerMin`. Two fighters with eight fights each can have wildly
different amounts of observed evidence if one finishes in the first round and the other
goes the distance. Minutes capture that; a fight count does not. Shrinkage weight is
26 minutes, roughly three full fights.

---

## 6. Weight cutting: fighters manage their own weight

**Ambiguity.** Weight management must matter without becoming a repetitive chore, and
there must be no consequence free maximum cut button.

**Decision.** A weekly `manageWalkingWeight` pass drifts each fighter's walking weight
toward what they can actually make, based on age, Longevity, accumulated cut stress and
build. When even that stops working the fighter moves up a division, vacating any title
they hold. Pushing a cut harder buys nothing except a higher chance of arriving depleted or
missing weight.

This was found through simulation. The first model made aging champions miss weight almost
every time, which silently downgraded nearly every title fight to a catchweight and froze
title pictures across the world. Missing weight now costs a purse forfeit and title
eligibility, and it happens at a believable rate.

---

## 7. Longevity is separate from Ovr and mostly permanent

Six internal wear components: neurological, facial, joint, body, weight cut and recovery.
The interface shows one Longevity number plus a plain language health report with no
graphic medical detail. Rest recovers most components; the neurological component is
72% permanent by configuration, which is why a long career of hard fights cannot be trained
away. Longevity is never included in Ovr.

---

## 8. Pot is a projection, not a magnet

Pot runs the same development model forward many times with independent variation in
growth rate, decline rate and hidden ceiling, then takes a configurable optimistic
percentile of projected peak Ovr. Development never consults Pot. A fighter can fall short
of it or pass it, and it is recomputed annually as new evidence arrives. Both the path
count and the percentile are player settings.

---

## 9. Scouting fog is stable, not jittery

Displayed estimates for fighters you do not control are derived from a hash of the fighter
id and the save seed, so the same fighter always shows the same estimate. Improving
scouting narrows the uncertainty band without moving the hidden value. A fighter does not
appear to get better or worse because you looked at them more closely.

---

## 10. Championship bouts are booked before anything else

**Problem found in simulation.** General matchmaking absorbed the top contenders into
ordinary bouts on nearer cards, so champions had nobody available to face and title
pictures never resolved. Over five simulated years only three title fights happened.

**Decision.** A dedicated `bookTitleFights` pass runs first each week, reserving the
champion and the highest ranked available contender on the next suitable card before
general matchmaking touches anyone. Combined with title stripping for a champion who cannot
defend for 550 days, interim titles at 300 days, and interim champion promotion, the same
five year run now produces 34 title fights and 7 title changes.

Card seeding is also interleaved across divisions by rank tier. Walking one division to
exhaustion before starting the next produced entire cards of a single weight class.

---

## 11. A decades long save must stay small

**Problem found in simulation.** Storing the full play by play for every fight forever
produced a 115 MB save after three years.

**Decision.** `pruneHistory` keeps complete event streams for the player's own fights
always, and for the 40 most recent fights otherwise. Older fights keep their result, round
summaries, scorecards and full statistics, which is everything the record books, fighter
pages and history pages actually read. Deep history additionally drops per round stat
lines. A five year world now sits around 35 MB, and the fight page states plainly when a
play by play has been trimmed rather than showing an empty panel.

---

## 12. Declining a fight is a real choice

Consequences are graded by reason. Declining while injured costs nothing at all. Declining
a bout on under 18 days notice costs almost nothing. Objecting to genuinely unreasonable
terms is taken on board. Repeated refusals without a reason escalate through relationship
damage, worse subsequent offers, public criticism in the fictional news system, and finally
release risk while fights are still owed on the contract.

---

## 13. Coach mode is advisory by construction

Fighters own their decisions. They can refuse a recommended camp plan, take a fight against
advice, ask for individual attention, object to hard sparring or favouritism, request a
different corner, decide to change divisions, and leave for a rival gym. Each of those
arrives as an inbox message with explicit choices whose consequences are stated, and
several of them make the room worse for everyone else if you accommodate one fighter. There
is no single victory condition; achievements accumulate and play continues indefinitely.

---

## 14. Emergent attributes, not extra ratings

The six ratings are closed. Concepts such as power, chin, speed and takedown defense are
produced in `effective.ts` by blending the six differently per contested domain, then
adjusting for physicals, stance, position, fatigue, damage, injury, camp state, game plan
and form on the night. Power, for example, comes from a striking heavy blend scaled by
division power factor and build leverage, and expresses itself through knockdown
probability and damage magnitude rather than as a stored number.

Style tendencies are 0 to 1 behavioural dials that shape which actions a fighter chooses.
They are never performance ratings and are derived for real fighters from their published
strike target and strike position breakdowns.

---

## 15. Judging follows the criteria in order

Effective striking and grappling is the primary criterion. Aggression is consulted only
when the primary is effectively even, and cage control only when the first two are. Each
of the three judges carries a small persistent lean toward grappling or damage plus per
round perception noise. They are imperfect observers, not incompetent ones. 10-8 rounds
require a dominant margin, 10-7 rounds are configurable, and 10-10 is off by default.

---

## 16. Event and card structure

Cards land on Saturdays. Rather than stepping a fixed number of days and snapping forward,
which silently inflates the real gap, the scheduler walks every Saturday and keeps a
fraction of them. That makes a fractional events per month setting behave exactly as
written. Card sizes are 10 to 11 bouts, and the best fight is promoted to close the card
after booking rather than being fixed at booking time.

---

## 17. Interface

Compact tables, small type, tabular numerals, sticky headers, sortable columns, no
decorative hero sections inside a save, no modals for primary data, and no images. The
global advance control is context aware: its primary button becomes answering a decision,
going to a weigh in, or entering the fight, and auto advance always stops when something
needs an answer.

Play by play uses the stored event stream as its own replay source, so live text,
round by round and instant presentation are three views of one deterministic simulation
rather than three code paths.

# Expansion decisions

Added during the expansion pass. Same rule as above: every technically ambiguous
requirement, and the decision taken for it.

---

## 18. The advance control only asks for answers that are still open

**Problem.** Accepting a fight offer from the offer screen left the inbox message that
carried it marked as needing action, so the advance button kept reading "Answer 1" for a
fight the player had already taken and already built a camp for.

**Decision.** `messageNeedsAction` is now the single source of truth, and it looks through
to the thing the message points at. A message linked to an offer needs an answer only
while that offer is open; a message linked to a bout only while that bout is scheduled.
Every path that closes an offer, including accepting, declining, expiry, withdrawal and
the matchmaker running out of patience, resolves the linked messages with a recorded
reason. The advance control, the sidebar badge and the inbox filter all read the same
predicate, so they cannot disagree. When nothing is open the primary button reads
"Advance", "Advance to weigh in" or "Advance to fight day" depending on where the career
is.

---

## 19. Women's divisions are first class, and each division has its own profile

Three contested women's divisions plus one retired one are configured, with 48 real
athletes ingested from the same compliant source. Every division carries its own physical
priors, age distribution, roster size, finish rate target, knockdown scale, submission
scale, walk around assumption, draw weight, and the years it was contested. No two
divisions share a prior set, and there is a test that asserts it.

`divisionsInYear` exists so a historical start can be told which divisions existed at the
time. The historical world state itself is not populated: see the remaining work.

---

## 20. Fighter status is derived, not stored twice

The stored `activityStatus` is the roster relationship with the promotion. The displayed
status is richer, because being booked, injured, medically suspended or a free agent are
different things from being released. Deriving the display status from the stored fields
means it can never contradict the record, and every simulation check keeps reading the one
stored field. Each verdict carries whether it came from the source, from the simulation, or
from inference.

---

## 21. Fighters do not all compete at the same rate

Twelve activity profiles, each with a target number of fights a year, a preferred
turnaround, a short notice willingness and a selectivity. Matchmaking asks
`willingToFight` before offering, so a fighter inside their own preferred turnaround or
already at their yearly target is a harder booking. Across three seeds the result is a
mean of 1.9 fights per active fighter per year with nobody exceeding four, which is the
distribution the specification asks for.

---

## 22. Attention and approval are separate numbers

Fame is not one popularity value. Recognition is how many people know the name;
favorability is how well liked; controversy is how much of the attention is negative;
hardcore respect and casual interest move differently from each other. A dull win raises
recognition slightly and lowers favorability. Public labels such as "boring winner",
"unmarketable elite fighter" and "polarizing star" are derived from those numbers rather
than stored, so they cannot drift out of agreement with the fighter underneath.

Drawing power is a composite of attention, approval and social reach, and it is what the
event business model reads. None of it touches Ovr.

---

## 23. Social media actions can fail

Thirteen actions, each with a base success chance modified by the personality dial that
governs it and by the fighter's actual credibility. A callout from someone on a losing run
reads as desperate and costs favorability. A joke without charisma does not land and gets
quoted back all week. Going silent is safe and slowly costs reach. Every action reports
what happened in plain language rather than only a number.

---

## 24. Hype is four audiences, not one

Hardcore, casual, regional and media interest are tracked separately, because they respond
to different things. A style clash moves hardcore interest; star power and finish
reputation move casual interest; controversy moves media interest; a local fighter moves
regional interest. Hype builds toward fight week rather than sitting flat, and a withdrawal
is recorded as a negative moment on the timeline.

---

## 25. Every business figure declares what kind of number it is

Attendance, gate, pay per view, audiences and profit each carry a kind: verified, reported
estimate, simulated, or unknown. Everything the model produces is marked simulated and the
event page says so in plain language. A card without a pay per view is `unknown` rather
than zero, because zero is a claim and unknown is the truth.

---

## 26. Bonuses are earned, not handed out

Fight of the Night requires a fight that was actually competitive and eventful, not simply
the highest scoring one on a weak card, and a card that produced nothing worthy gets no
award and says so. When there is no Fight of the Night, a third performance bonus is
awarded instead. Selection weighs the method, how quickly or how late the finish came,
whether the winner had been dropped first, and whether a title was at stake. A bonus moves
favorability, hardcore respect and the matchmaker relationship, so it is a career event
rather than only money.

---

## 27. The calendar is built from real Saturdays

Rather than stepping a fixed number of days, the scheduler walks every Saturday inside the
horizon and decides whether it carries a card. The verdict is derived from the date and the
save seed rather than drawn fresh, because the scheduler runs weekly and a fresh draw kept
re-rolling a skipped weekend until it was finally taken, which silently inflated the event
count. Numbered card placement uses an explicit deficit correction against the annual
target rather than an independent probability, so the yearly split lands where it should
instead of drifting.

Card shapes are drawn at creation and stored, so a card knows what it is meant to be before
any bout is booked. Four separate views of a card are kept, because they legitimately
differ: announced, currently scheduled, at the weigh in, and actually contested.

---

## 28. Ranked and unranked fighters are interleaved on a card

**Problem found in simulation.** Ranked fighters were seeded first and there are far more
of them than there are card slots, so unranked fighters were frozen out of the sport
permanently. A player who fell out of the rankings stopped receiving offers entirely.

**Decision.** Seeding interleaves two ranked fighters with three unranked ones, which is
roughly how a real card divides between its main card and its preliminaries. Champions are
still seeded first and title bouts are still made before anything else.

---

## 29. The player is never assigned a fight

Every fight the player takes arrives as an offer they can accept, negotiate or refuse,
including short notice replacement work. The automatic replacement finder skips the
player's fighter entirely. Volunteering for short notice work on the offer screen raises
matchmaker goodwill, which is the intended route into those bouts.

---

## 30. A missed weight changes who can win the title, not whether it is a title fight

**The ambiguity.** A championship bout where one fighter misses weight is still a
championship bout for the other one. The previous behaviour dropped title status entirely,
which is simple but wrong: it meant a challenger could miss weight and thereby protect the
champion from ever losing the belt.

**Decision.** The bout keeps its championship status and records which fighters forfeited
their claim, in `titleIneligibleFighterIds`. One rule settles it:

1. The belt goes to the winner only if the winner made weight.
2. Nobody who missed weight holds the belt afterwards.
3. Every other case leaves the title vacant.

This is stated as the rule this game applies rather than as a claim about any promotion's
published rulebook, because that document is not part of the compliant source set. It is
internally coherent and covers all four combinations. The five cases are unit tested and
the fight page states whose title is at stake.

---

## 31. One predicate decides whether a belt is on the line

**Bug found by writing a test for a system that had never run.** An interim bout sets
`isTitleFight` to false and `isInterimTitleFight` to true. Roughly a dozen places read only
`isTitleFight` while meaning "a belt is on the line", so an interim championship was
scheduled for three rounds, contracted at the non title weight allowance, paid no
championship money, skipped by the news, fame and ranking point systems, and, fatally,
discarded by `applyTitleOutcome` before it could record anything.

**Decision.** `isChampionshipBout(x)` in `types/fight.ts` is the single predicate for "a
belt is on the line". `isTitleFight` on its own now means the undisputed title, and only
the code that moves the undisputed title reads the raw flag.

The lesson generalizes: a flag pair where one member implies the negation of the other
invites exactly this failure. The predicate is the fix, not a comment.

---

## 32. A fighter is created at a weight they can actually cut to

**Bug found in simulation.** `manageWalkingWeight` caps a fighter's walk around weight at
what their own frame can safely take off, but fighter creation used the divisional norm
alone. New fighters therefore started carrying more than they could cut and drifted down
over the following months at six percent of the gap per week. Measured across every
division at save start, 23.7 percent of weight cuts failed.

Separately, the cut model double counted two of its own terms. `build.cutDifficulty` both
divided the healthy cut and multiplied the strain, so it landed squared and punished hard
cutting builds several times over: light heavyweight, where those builds cluster, was
missing weight at 44.7 percent. `wear.weightCut` did exactly the same thing, and that one
compounded over a career, so a busy champion who had taken years of cuts was missing weight
in a quarter of their title bouts.

**Decision.** `sustainableWalkingWeight` is the single definition of how much a fighter
carries, creation clamps down to it, and each term in the strain formula is counted once.
Build difficulty and accumulated cut wear live in `healthyCutFor`, which is also what
`manageWalkingWeight` reads, so a fighter whose cutting capacity has degraded carries less
and eventually moves up rather than repeatedly failing on the scale.

The wear term deliberately stays at its original depth. Deepening it so that wear mattered
more, having removed the duplicate, pushed enough fighters into division changes to vacate
titles noticeably more often, which inflated the title fight rate out of its band. One
channel at the right strength beats two channels at any strength.

**Effect.** Under identical ideal camp conditions, the miss rate across every division fell
from 23.7 percent to 0.2 percent for a normal bout and from 26.3 to 0.6 percent at a
championship limit. In a running world, where camps are short and cuts are worse, weight
misses in title bouts fell from 25 percent to under 4 percent. Title fights per division
per year fell from 2.36 back to 1.93, inside its band, because a missed weight now vacates
a title and each spurious vacancy was generating a spurious title fight.

## 33. Acceptance metrics are rates, not counts

**Incorrect test assumption, found when the harness ran at a larger scale.** The vacancy
row counted long vacancy spells across a whole run and compared that count against a fixed
band, so a ten year run was held to the same number as a five year run and the row reported
run length as much as world health. It also walked gaps between consecutive reigns only,
which silently ignored a title still vacant when the run ended.

Two more divisor errors sat alongside it: per division rates divided by all twelve
configured divisions including the one that is no longer contested, and new reigns were
counted as total reigns minus a hardcoded division count, which assumed every configured
division had a champion on day one.

**Decision.** Per division rates divide by the divisions actually contested. A title change
is a reign that began after the save started, compared by date. Long vacancies are reported
per division per year against a band derived from the metric's meaning, that a given
division should sit longer than six months without a champion less than once a decade, with
the raw spell count printed underneath as a diagnostic.

## 34. One constant sets the title fight rate

Correcting the divisors in decision 33 exposed a real imbalance that the broken metric had
been hiding: title fights were running at 2.1 to 2.4 per division per year against a one to
two band.

**Two wrong guesses before the right answer, both worth recording.** The first was that the
weight miss loop was to blame. It contributed, and fixing the double counted cut wear did
bring the figure from 2.36 down to 1.93 on a single seed, but the multi seed mean stayed at
2.06. The second was that title bouts were escaping the champion turnaround gate, because
card seeding applied it only when the champion was the seeded fighter, so a contender could
pull a champion into a title fight from the other side of the pairing. That was a genuine
defect and is fixed, but closing it moved the mean from 2.06 to 2.11. It made champions more
entrenched rather than title fights rarer.

**The actual cause is arithmetic.** The gate is the binding constraint, not a floor that
rarely bites. Measured utilization runs at about 75 percent of the ceiling the gate implies,
so the rate is close to `0.75 * 365 / turnaround`. At 130 days that is 2.1, which is a
champion defending every four months. `CHAMPION_TURNAROUND_DAYS` is now 180, a titleholder
fighting about twice a year, and both booking paths read it.

**The lesson is about diagnosis, not about the constant.** Two plausible structural
explanations were each investigated for longer than the measurement that settled it would
have taken. When a metric is set by a single rate limit, compute what that limit permits
before looking for a leak.

---

## 35. One gate decides every championship booking

Four independent code paths could put a belt on the line: the weekly title pass, ordinary
card seeding, the replacement finder and the player offer path. Each carried its own idea of
who was eligible. Card seeding accepted any challenger ranked in the top six with no check on
whether they were coming off a loss, and only the weekly pass checked whether the division
already had a championship bout scheduled, so two live title fights for the same belt were
reachable.

`titleShotEligibility` in `title-eligibility.ts` is now the single answer. It returns a
verdict, a list of named blockers, and the sentence explaining the selection, which is stored
on the bout and shown on the offer. Standing is satisfied by one of several explicit routes
rather than by a ranking threshold alone: a top five ranking, a ranked fighter on an unbeaten
run, a win over the current number one contender, a former champion on a comeback run, the
interim champion challenging for the undisputed belt, or a champion arriving from another
division. Naming the routes is what lets the game explain a title shot that a pure ranking
check would have called anomalous.

**Interim belts require a stated reason.** `interimTitleJustification` returns one of five
named causes and refuses when none applies. An interim championship is never created because
the scheduler could not find the champion a date.

---

## 36. A callout leaves an object behind

The callout system worked and reached nothing. It changed a relationship, and the matchmaker
read a pressure value that decayed over 120 days. If the two fighters were not seeded onto the
same card in the same weekly pass, the callout evaporated with no explanation. A player could
call somebody out, get a favourable answer, and never hear about it again.

A `MatchupInterest` is the missing object. It is created when a callout gets any answer other
than a flat refusal, it persists in the save, and it is re-evaluated every week against the
world. A blocked matchup is not deleted: it is marked blocked with the named blocker, and it
becomes eligible again the moment the blocker clears. The weekly pass converts an eligible
interest with real backing into an actual fight offer whose stated reason is the callout.

**Blocked is a state, not a failure.** The distinction matters because the player can see it.
"They are already booked for another fight, the matchup can be revisited afterwards" is a
different message from silence, and it is the difference between a system that feels
connected and one that feels fake.

**Rivalry contributes on its own.** `matchupPull` reads the interest and the relationship
together, so two fighters with genuine history are matched even when neither said anything
publicly, and a current training partner is pushed out of contention entirely.

---

## 37. A division move changes eligibility, not a label

`eligibleDivisions` was written by three call sites and read by none. Matchmaking keyed on
`divisionId` alone, so a move updated the label while open offers at the old weight stayed
live in the inbox and no debut opponent was ever sought. The fighter joined the unranked pool
and waited to be noticed.

A move now withdraws every open offer outside the new division, closes the outgoing division
spell in `divisionHistory` so the record of what was achieved at the old weight survives, and
seeds a real prioritised debut matchup scaled to the standing the fighter brings with them.
`createFightOffer` refuses outright when the two fighters are in different divisions, so no
later path can reintroduce the old behaviour.

**A champion moving weight picks a path.** `assessChampionMove` returns one of six outcomes
in a deliberate order: a direct championship bout against the destination champion first, an
interim belt only when the shared justification gate agrees, then a contender bout, then a
single debut fight. The original championship is kept temporarily, kept for a double champion
attempt, or vacated, and `enforceAbsentChampions` strips a belt held by somebody who left the
division and never came back. A champion cannot silently disappear from a division while
remaining its champion forever.

---

## 38. Measure the player's experience, not the rules

The title shot work in decision 35 was tested two ways: the eligibility rules in isolation,
and the world invariants over a simulated year. Both passed. The acceptance run reported 1.62
title fights per division per year, inside the band. Every one of those measurements was
about the world. None of them answered the only question a player asks, which is whether a
title shot ever arrives for them.

It did not. `tools/title-shot-probe.ts` walks a real career forward, answering offers and
fighting the fights, and reports why a title fight did not happen on every weekly pass. The
first run said zero title offers in six careers of six years. The cause was not the title
logic at all.

**Two of the three causes were in the measurement, and finding that out was the point.** The
first probe counted offers without accepting them, so the player never fought and never
climbed; best rank was exactly 7 in all six runs, which is the starting rank. The second
accepted offers but never signed a contract renewal, and a fighter with no active contract is
refused by every offer path, so the career froze. Correcting only that took the same three
seeds from 11 ordinary offers to 52, from a best rank of 5 to a best rank of 1, and from no
title shots to two.

**The third cause was real.** A fighter holding an ordinary open offer was unavailable to the
weekly title pass, so being offered a preliminary bout on Monday silently cost a top
contender the title shot decided on Tuesday, and the belt went elsewhere for another six
months. `isChampionshipBooking` now relaxes the ordinary offer and the offer cooldown for a
championship booking only. Health, an existing booking and a competing championship offer
still block, and the player's routine offer is withdrawn when the title shot replaces it.

**A rejected fix is worth recording too.** The same flag initially bypassed `willingToFight`
as well, on the reasoning that nobody turns down a championship. That made every division
book title fights faster, which crowded the player out rather than helping them: best rank
across three seeds went from 1, 1, 2 to 7, 1, 7. The bypass was reverted. A three seed sample
was also not enough to tell a regression from noise, which is why the figures above are over
eight.

**Being out of contract is now stated, not implied.** It produced no symptom other than
fights quietly never arriving. The career state names the consequence: no fights can be
offered until a new deal is signed.
